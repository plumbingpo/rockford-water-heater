A gas water heater is a refreshingly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. Most failures trace back to one of those parts. In Rockford, IL, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Rockford Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Rockford call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. Call Rockford Water Heater at (815) 205-5095 at the first sign of trouble.

The Benefits of Professional Gas Water Heater Services

Start with the hazards. Fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust laced with carbon monoxide come with every gas water heater. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and working through them by guesswork is slow and costly.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. Instead of trial and error, we confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with proper tools. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. When a replacement is needed, we size the new heater by first-hour rating, handle the permit where one is required, and add the expansion tank, sediment trap, drain pan, or seismic straps your home needs. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and efficiency
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
