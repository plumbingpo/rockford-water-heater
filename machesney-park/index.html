How Machesney Park Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
