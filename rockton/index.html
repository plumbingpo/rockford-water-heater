Keeping Your Rockton Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Common Water Heater Problems in Rockton

Well before a tank floods a Rockton garage, it almost always sends signals. Look out for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
